# PLAN

**Everything here must keep working in s&box.** The engine is shared source, not a port:
`sbox-library/Skafinity/Code/Engine/**` compiles into both the s&box library and the web wasm bundle.
So every row below is bound by the same rules — the engine stays framework-free (`System`,
`System.Collections.Generic`, `System.Text` only; no `Sandbox.*`, no Emscripten-isms), anything
web-specific goes in `wasm/Exports.cs`, and any new `Config` field lands in `Cfg.To`/`Cfg.From`
(+ `Cfg.Size`) or it won't survive the JS boundary. `SkafinityPlayer.cs` and `Code/UI/` are
s&box-only and must still compile and drive the engine after each row.

Seed compatibility is **not** preserved: old `vibe:tag:n` seeds are expected to break, and the
audio a seed renders is expected to change from commit to commit. That is accepted — no
back-compat shim, no golden-audio contract, and a changed render is never a regression. The
only reproducibility that matters is *within* a build (web and s&box agreeing, and a pure
refactor proving it moved nothing).

| 72 | The chordal voices leap a tenth when the progression moves, because nothing inverts a chord | Found with `--score` while chasing a section-boundary complaint, and it is audible at exactly the same moments (a chord change that coincides with a new section reads as "it jumped"). Every chordal voice builds its chord upward from the progression's degree — `ChordMidis(baseMidi, chord)`, over `triBase = _rootMidi + 12` for the guitar and `+24` for the keys — so the chord's register is wherever its ROOT degree happens to fall and the same shape simply slides. On `100300746a85005a209340c7c07a9:mpdk7cup:1` across bar 12→13 the keys drop 68/72/75 → 58/61/65 and the guitar 56/63 → 46/53: no common tone, a tenth of parallel motion, every time. A real player inverts to stay in one register and keeps common tones — that is what makes a comp sound like one part rather than a chord calculator. The fix is a voice-leading step between the voicing and the render: pick the inversion of the next chord nearest the previous one (octave-shift each degree to minimise total motion), applied once where the degrees are resolved so every chordal voice gets it. Watch two things — the bass must NOT be voice-led (it plays roots, that is the point), and metal's bare power chords have only two notes so the inversion choice is narrower. Re-measure `--levels` afterwards: moving a comp's average register changes how much of it survives its low-pass. |

Note the render digests do NOT cover this: they run on the engine-only harness, so a build-flag change needs `make test` and a listen, not a hash. |

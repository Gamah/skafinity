# PLAN

**Everything here must keep working in s&box.** The engine is shared source, not a port:
`sbox-library/Skafinity/Code/Engine/**` compiles into both the s&box library and the web wasm bundle.
So every row below is bound by the same rules — the engine stays framework-free (`System`,
`System.Collections.Generic`, `System.Text` only; no `Sandbox.*`, no Emscripten-isms), anything
web-specific goes in `wasm/Exports.cs`, and any new `Config` field lands in `Cfg.To`/`Cfg.From`
(+ `Cfg.Size`) or it won't survive the JS boundary. `SkafinityPlayer.cs` and `Code/UI/` are
s&box-only and must still compile and drive the engine after each row.

Seed compatibility is **not** preserved: old `vibe:tag:n` seeds are expected to break, and the
audio a seed renders is expected to change from commit to commit. That is accepted — no
back-compat shim, no golden-audio contract, and a changed render is never a regression. The
only reproducibility that matters is *within* a build (web and s&box agreeing, and a pure
refactor proving it moved nothing).

| 99 | One authority arranges every part at once, and nothing melodic comes from a table | Every voice picks its figure from its own small authored table and no voice knows what any other is playing. The single exception is `_riffOnsets` — the chordal voice renders first and the bass reads it — and it is the only lockup in the engine that is not a coincidence (metal bass on comp 93% of onsets, punk 98%; everything else is two tables that happen to agree, e.g. pop's pad at 100% on the kick against ska's skank at 1%). The tables are also a hard ceiling: over 500 songs per genre the WHOLE rhythm section has 12 distinct states in punk, 18 in metal, 24 in pop, 30 in ska, 32 in country, 48 in rock — one punk song in nine repeats the previous one's comp + bass + groove with only the key and the tune moved, and randomness cannot touch that because it is a table-size limit rather than a draw. Shape: an arranger pass in `Compose.cs` between planning and rendering. Tune FIRST, then one rhythmic skeleton per section (accents, phrase seams, band stops) published the way `_energy`/`_feel` already are, then each voice GENERATES its line as a role against the skeleton and the tune — bass agreeing with the kick at cadences and moving where the tune holds, comp playing complementary rhythm rather than its own. `GenreProfile` keeps saying *how* (density, register, subdivision, offbeat-or-downbeat, how hard the bass locks to the kick) and never *what*, so ska's skank stays offbeat by rule. **Drums stay table-driven and that is not an inconsistency** — they are not melodic and the grooves are fitted to the Groove MIDI corpus, so the kit is the measured reference the arranger writes against. **The failure mode is the exact opposite of today's and it has to be measured, not hoped for: a shared skeleton makes every part play the same rhythm, which is worse than parts that ignore each other.** The suite needs a distinctness check alongside the cohesion one — per voice-pair onset agreement AND distinct-part counts across a seed sweep — and today's numbers are the baseline to beat on both sides: bass-on-kick 27/47/41/89/45/53% and comp-on-snare 1/31/60/16/70/10% across genres 0–5. Joint generation makes draw order a contract, so the "every genre pulls the same number of values" rule gets stricter; re-bless the digests. |
| 99 | The tune is a stepwise walk with three note lengths, and half of it is derived from the other half | Measured over 500 chorus tunes per genre: **0%** of onsets fall off the eighth grid, note lengths are only 24/48/96 ticks (a quarter is ~50% of all notes in every genre), the first degree is only ever 0, 2 or 4 (33/33/33), the last is the tonic **100%** of the time, a leap is always exactly a third and never anything else, and 10–18% of notes sit pinned at the `Clamp(-2, 9)` ends, which is where the 7–12% of repeated adjacent notes come from. The answer repeats the call's rhythm 100% of the time by design and is literally the call transposed down one degree 51% (metal) to 80% (punk) of the time. So every tune is unique — 500/500 distinct in four genres — and every tune is the same *kind* of tune, which is what "samey with different notes" is. Three separable levers: widen the rhythmic vocabulary (rests as first-class cells, sixteenths, dotted values, ties across the beat — the 32nd row already proved `Timing` needs nothing); vary the answer (repeat with a different ending, sequence it up, invert it) instead of always call−1; and reflect off the register ends rather than clamping. Punk and pop are worst and for a structural reason — their tunes are ~13 notes, so only 258 and 320 distinct rhythms exist in 500 songs. **Same caution as the arranger row: widening the vocabulary must not converge the tunes** — the check is that distinct-rhythm and distinct-contour counts go UP while pairwise onset overlap (31–40% today) does not, so measure both before and after. |
| 99 | The genre is not in the tune's seed, so different genres sing the same melody | `DrawTunes` seeds off `{_tag}:tune:chorus` where `_tag` is `"{tag}:{n}"`, and the genre reaches the tune only through `density`, `leap` and `cycle`. Where two genres' densities are close the draws mostly agree, so the tunes come back byte-identical: Rock↔Country **50%** of the time over 500 songs, Punk↔Pop **53%**, Ska↔Country 10%. Same `n`, different genre, different key and kit, literally the same melody — which is most of why the roster reads as one band. The fix is one string, and it is worth doing even if the arranger row lands first, because whatever generates the tune still has to be seeded per genre. Cheap; re-bless. |
| 99 | A genre's song form has exactly ONE state, so every song of a genre is the same arc | `BuildStructure(genre)` is `new( GenreProfile.For(genre).Form )` — no `Rng`, no per-song input at all. Next to the layers above it: the tune has 500 states in 500 songs, punk's whole rhythm section has 12, **the form has 1**. Every ska-punk song is 9 sections and 60 bars with the hemiola on the second verse and the half-time bridge in the same place; every country and pop song modulates up a tone for the final chorus, every time, forever. Length varies only with tempo. The six forms are also near-identical to each other — all six are Intro 4 → Verse 8 → … → Chorus 8 → Ending 4 with exactly 3 choruses and 2 verses and every section 4 or 8 bars, so the genre difference is only WHICH optional section appears (PreChorus / Bridge / Breakdown / Solo). In an endless stream that is the same 60-bar arc on loop. **Randomising the form is the wrong fix and the row is not asking for it** — a form is genre identity (which is why it lives in `GenreProfile`) and punk with a 16-bar solo is not punk. What is wanted is a FAMILY of forms per genre plus per-song variation in the details: section lengths (8 vs 12 vs 16), whether the optional section appears, a doubled last chorus, a truncated final verse, whether the key lift happens at all. Invariants to hold: choruses identical to each other, every section a multiple of 4 bars (the hypermeasure — it is why the ending is 4 and not 2), and a resolved ending. Sequence it with the arranger row rather than independently: the skeleton is drawn per section, and `RenderTune`'s section-shorter-than-the-tune handling already exists, so variable section lengths land on machinery that is ready for them. Re-bless. |
| 40 | The cymbals are not tuned, hats included | The ride carries three stacked edits (`StrokeLevelRide` 0.45, `RingTau` knee 3500, `BowStrike` bump 4200) and the hats two (`KitNuance.OpenHatDurMin/Max` 0.26–0.42, `HatBalance` 0.288). Every one of them landed off a single listening note on a single seed, and none has been swept across the six genres or against a section that is dense rather than sparse. **The crash has deliberately not been touched through any of it**, so it is now the least-examined voice in the kit and quite possibly the loud one. Levers by reach: the three ride constants above, the open-hat band, then `HatBalance`/`RideBalance`/`CrashBalance` in `skafinity.config.json` (no rebuild). Judge the voice with `--cymbal` and the mix with the band + duty-cycle read (CLAUDE.md) — whole-mix RMS is what sent three rounds of this after the wrong voice. |
| 35 | The melody has no dynamics at all, and the reason it has none is that the ones it had were the drummer's | `NoteGain` no longer takes a tick, so a pitched note's level is its cell velocity times the section's energy — and every authored cell carries velocity 1, so **every note of every tune is the same loudness**. That is the right floor to build from (the thing it replaced set a melody's level by where in the bar it landed, off drum-measured weights, which stepped the lead 3 dB a note in rock and 5 in pop) but a flat melody is not the destination. What a singer or a player actually does is shape a PHRASE: lean into the note the line lands on, ease off the pickup, come down over a resolution. That is the same axis `BendBias` already reads — note length against position within each half of the phrase, so a call and answer lands twice — so the shape is already computed and already proven to be the right one; what is missing is a velocity coming off it. The obvious home is `Melody.Draw`, which knows the whole phrase and already writes the answer against the call, so it can author velocities into the tune's cells the way it authors degrees; `RenderTune` then needs nothing. Two things to hold to. It must come off the TUNE and not off the grid, or it is the metric accent again wearing a different name — the test is whether moving a note to a different beat changes its level (it must not) while moving it to a different place in the phrase does. And it is the melody's, not the band's: the comp is a bed and a bed that swells every two bars is a bed you can hear thinking. Re-bless, and re-measure `--levels` — a phrase envelope changes a voice's mean level, and `LeadLevel` is a measured number against a +2 dB target. |
| 30 | The genre roster is six, and the obvious missing ones are the cousins of the genres already here | Wanted: **two-tone**, **reggae** and **R&B** as genres in their own right, and an exploration of whether **rock** and **pop** are each really one genre. All of them are the same shape of question — the current six are broad umbrellas, and the retune of genre 0 showed that an umbrella genre gets tuned to exactly one era by accident and then reads as mistuned to everyone who meant a different one. Two-tone is the direct case: it is neither the first-wave shuffle nor the ska-punk blast, it is the fast straight 2 Tone sound with its own harmony and its own production, and today there is nowhere for it. Reggae is the other end of the same lineage (one-drop, swung hats, a much slower band — 12/8 makes it partly dependent on the non-4/4 row) and is where the *current* ska profile's melodic bass, roomy mix and laid-back form would find their proper home, so the retune's discarded tables are the seed of it rather than a loss. **R&B is the lineage's HEAD**, and that is what makes it worth having: the ska stroke came out of 1940s–50s American R&B and jump blues, where the boogie-woogie piano vamp and its swung eighths were simulated by a guitar chop on the offbeat — so adding R&B gives the engine the *source* of a feel three of its genres inherited, and the shuffle band that ska just gave up gets a genre that actually means it. It is also the roster's worst umbrella and cannot be added without picking an era first: shuffle-era R&B (jump blues, the Rosco Gordon boogie, horn sections, 12-bar) and modern R&B (90s/00s neo-soul and contemporary R&B — slow, quantised, sixteenth-based, programmed kit, keys-and-vocal led) share a name and almost nothing else. They are further apart than any two genres currently in the engine, and averaging them into one profile is exactly the mistake genre 0 made across four decades of ska. **Under the era lean (`CLAUDE.md`), take the modern one** — 90s/00s R&B sits with the rest of the roster, where jump blues would be the one genre in the engine from before the Beatles. Note honestly what that costs: the tidy argument for R&B was that it is where ska's shuffle came from and would be a genre that finally *means* the shuffle band ska gave up, and that argument is for the era being passed over. Modern R&B does not swing; it is straight sixteenths with the feel in the programming, so it declares `SwingChance = 0` like everything else and country keeps the shuffle alone. It brings a different thing instead, which the engine has never had: a genre whose groove is SIXTEENTH-based rather than eighth-based, and whose kit is programmed rather than played — which is likely to be the real work, and is worth checking against `Pattern`/`Timing` before committing to it (the 32nd-note row proved the grid is fine; what is unproven is whether the voices read a sixteenth-led groove naturally or whether that is a new `CompStyle`). The jump-blues era stays a legitimate future row, just not this one. Rock and pop are the umbrellas most likely to be hiding two distinct genres each; work out what the split actually is before assuming it (the tempo-band study row is the same kind of exercise and should probably be done first, since it is the tool for telling two eras apart). **The mechanism is append-only and cheap** — genre is the first base-36 char, so 36 genres fit with no wire change; `GenreProfile.Profiles` is an array indexed by genre id and appends without shifting anything; each genre's instrument grid is already independent. **The hard part is the anti-overlap rule, and it gets combinatorially worse.** CLAUDE.md caps any two genres at one shared progression and one shared scale, and the suite asserts it — six genres already required pruning I–V–vi–IV out of four tables. Close cousins are exactly the case that rule punishes: ska-punk and two-tone, or two halves of a split rock, will collide on the first table anyone writes. Before adding a genre, decide whether the cap survives as-is, becomes a per-pair rule (cousins may overlap more, distant genres may not), or is replaced by a distinctness measure over the whole profile rather than over the harmony tables alone — because the real answer is that two-tone differs from ska-punk in feel, form, production and horn writing rather than in its chord choices, and a rule that only watches harmony cannot see that. Each new genre is a full table set (scales, progressions, voicings, bass, comp figures, keys figures, grooves, form, lead style, accents, mix) plus a `--levels` pass to measure its balances. Re-bless the digests only if the matrix is extended to cover the new genres — adding a profile past the end of the array moves nothing that exists. |
| 14 | Non-4/4 | `Timing` already carries `BeatsPerBar` and a `BeatGrouping` array (nothing reads the grouping yet), `ComposePlan` picks the meter in one place, and patterns are meter-agnostic now — a `Pattern` carries its own tick length and nothing in a voice assumes eight cells to a bar. 6/8 and 12/8 first (12/8 is the natural home of a one-drop shuffle; a country bass lesson in 6/8 turned up in the research).

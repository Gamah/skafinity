# PLAN

**Everything here must keep working in s&box.** The engine is shared source, not a port:
`sbox-library/Skafinity/Code/Engine/**` compiles into both the s&box library and the web wasm bundle.
So every row below is bound by the same rules — the engine stays framework-free (`System`,
`System.Collections.Generic`, `System.Text` only; no `Sandbox.*`, no Emscripten-isms), anything
web-specific goes in `wasm/Exports.cs`, and any new `Config` field lands in `Cfg.To`/`Cfg.From`
(+ `Cfg.Size`) or it won't survive the JS boundary. `SkafinityPlayer.cs` and `Code/UI/` are
s&box-only and must still compile and drive the engine after each row.

Seed compatibility is **not** preserved: old `vibe:tag:n` seeds are expected to break, and the
audio a seed renders is expected to change from commit to commit. That is accepted — no
back-compat shim, no golden-audio contract, and a changed render is never a regression. The
only reproducibility that matters is *within* a build (web and s&box agreeing, and a pure
refactor proving it moved nothing).

| 34 | Every drum fill is a constant stream of sixteenths, so a fill reads as busy rather than as a gesture | The listening note is precise and worth keeping precise: the fills are **on tempo and too busy**, not too fast — "full of 16ths or maybe even 32nds". `RenderFill` (`Drums/Groove.cs`) is why, and it is structural rather than a tuning value. It splits the fill span into beats and plays `per` evenly-spaced hits in **every** beat of it, where `per` is 4 by default, 8 (32nds) 30% of the time on fills of ≤2 beats, and 3 or 6 on the triplet roll — so the FLOOR is four hits a beat for the fill's whole length and there is no branch anywhere that plays fewer. A two-bar fill into a chorus is 32 unbroken, evenly-spaced, equally-loud hits, and that is the blast-beat read: nothing about it is fast, it simply never stops. Three separable things are missing. **A fill has a SHAPE** — real ones are two hits that accelerate into the downbeat, or three tom hits, or a single flam, or a sparse bar that fills up only at its end; this engine has exactly one shape, "occupy every subdivision", and `per` cannot express any of the others because it is one number applied to every beat. **A fill has DYNAMICS** — `RenderFill` calls `RenderSnare`/`RenderTom`/`RenderRide` directly and never goes through `NoteGain`/`MetricGain`, so it is the one part of the kit with no accent pattern and no energy scaling at all, which is what makes an even stream read as a wall rather than as a phrase; that is also a straight inconsistency with the rule that every voice routes its level through `NoteGain`. And **density is genre-independent today** while nothing else about the kit is: country's fill vocabulary is the train beat's ghosted snare, pop's is programmed and sparse, metal's is the wall, and one `per` serves all six. **The density is now MEASURED, so this row is a rewrite against a number rather than a retune by ear.** Row 24's pass over the dataset's fill performances — 236 of them in 4/4, rock alone 130 files over 204 bars — found that a real rock fill averages **13.2 hits per bar, i.e. ~3.3 per beat, across all four drums together**. The engine's FLOOR is 16/bar and its 32nd branch is 32/bar, so the quietest fill this engine can play is already busier than the average real one and its loud branch is ~2.4× it. The shape shows in the same histogram: the `e` and `a` sixteenths carry roughly half the occupancy of the beats and the `&`s (rock fill snare 23/22/22/23 against 40/30/47/33), so a real fill is **eighths with sixteenth ornament**, not a sixteenth grid — which is the concrete target to write to. The dynamics have a number too: fill hits off the beat measure **0.78** against beat 3, where `RenderFill` currently applies no weighting whatsoever. Country's fills corroborate its groove row (offbeat 1.12) on a healthier 27 files than its 2 beat performances. Re-bless. |
| 30 | The genre roster is six, and the obvious missing ones are the cousins of the genres already here | Wanted: **two-tone**, **reggae** and **R&B** as genres in their own right, and an exploration of whether **rock** and **pop** are each really one genre. All of them are the same shape of question — the current six are broad umbrellas, and the retune of genre 0 showed that an umbrella genre gets tuned to exactly one era by accident and then reads as mistuned to everyone who meant a different one. Two-tone is the direct case: it is neither the first-wave shuffle nor the ska-punk blast, it is the fast straight 2 Tone sound with its own harmony and its own production, and today there is nowhere for it. Reggae is the other end of the same lineage (one-drop, swung hats, a much slower band — 12/8 makes it partly dependent on the non-4/4 row) and is where the *current* ska profile's melodic bass, roomy mix and laid-back form would find their proper home, so the retune's discarded tables are the seed of it rather than a loss. **R&B is the lineage's HEAD**, and that is what makes it worth having: the ska stroke came out of 1940s–50s American R&B and jump blues, where the boogie-woogie piano vamp and its swung eighths were simulated by a guitar chop on the offbeat — so adding R&B gives the engine the *source* of a feel three of its genres inherited, and the shuffle band that ska just gave up gets a genre that actually means it. It is also the roster's worst umbrella and cannot be added without picking an era first: shuffle-era R&B (jump blues, the Rosco Gordon boogie, horn sections, 12-bar) and modern R&B (90s/00s neo-soul and contemporary R&B — slow, quantised, sixteenth-based, programmed kit, keys-and-vocal led) share a name and almost nothing else. They are further apart than any two genres currently in the engine, and averaging them into one profile is exactly the mistake genre 0 made across four decades of ska. **Under the era lean (`CLAUDE.md`), take the modern one** — 90s/00s R&B sits with the rest of the roster, where jump blues would be the one genre in the engine from before the Beatles. Note honestly what that costs: the tidy argument for R&B was that it is where ska's shuffle came from and would be a genre that finally *means* the shuffle band ska gave up, and that argument is for the era being passed over. Modern R&B does not swing; it is straight sixteenths with the feel in the programming, so it declares `SwingChance = 0` like everything else and country keeps the shuffle alone. It brings a different thing instead, which the engine has never had: a genre whose groove is SIXTEENTH-based rather than eighth-based, and whose kit is programmed rather than played — which is likely to be the real work, and is worth checking against `Pattern`/`Timing` before committing to it (the 32nd-note row proved the grid is fine; what is unproven is whether the voices read a sixteenth-led groove naturally or whether that is a new `CompStyle`). The jump-blues era stays a legitimate future row, just not this one. Rock and pop are the umbrellas most likely to be hiding two distinct genres each; work out what the split actually is before assuming it (the tempo-band study row is the same kind of exercise and should probably be done first, since it is the tool for telling two eras apart). **The mechanism is append-only and cheap** — genre is the first base-36 char, so 36 genres fit with no wire change; `GenreProfile.Profiles` is an array indexed by genre id and appends without shifting anything; each genre's instrument grid is already independent. **The hard part is the anti-overlap rule, and it gets combinatorially worse.** CLAUDE.md caps any two genres at one shared progression and one shared scale, and the suite asserts it — six genres already required pruning I–V–vi–IV out of four tables. Close cousins are exactly the case that rule punishes: ska-punk and two-tone, or two halves of a split rock, will collide on the first table anyone writes. Before adding a genre, decide whether the cap survives as-is, becomes a per-pair rule (cousins may overlap more, distant genres may not), or is replaced by a distinctness measure over the whole profile rather than over the harmony tables alone — because the real answer is that two-tone differs from ska-punk in feel, form, production and horn writing rather than in its chord choices, and a rule that only watches harmony cannot see that. Each new genre is a full table set (scales, progressions, voicings, bass, comp figures, keys figures, grooves, form, lead style, accents, mix) plus a `--levels` pass to measure its balances. Re-bless the digests only if the matrix is extended to cover the new genres — adding a profile past the end of the array moves nothing that exists. |
| 26 | Country bends correctly and too often — the bend rate is a floor, not a decision | The gesture itself is right and is not what this row touches: the bend lands on a note of the key and is mostly released, which is what the bend work just shipped, and the listening note says so — "it does sound right, just a little too often". This is the RATE alone. `Expression.Expr` gives country's lead `bend: MathF.Max( knob, 0.45f ), bendDepth: 2f`, so **at least 45% of every note long enough to carry one** (`BendMinSeconds`, 0.30 s) bends a whole step, and the `BENDINESS` knob can only push that higher — there is no position on the slider at which country bends rarely. The reason a smaller constant is probably the wrong repair is that a flat per-note chance cannot express *where* a player bends: a bender or a pedal steel bends into a phrase ending and on the long held note, and passes straight through the run — so the likely shape is a weighting (phrase position, note length relative to its neighbours, whether the note is the tone the tune resolves onto) rather than a lower number. Whatever replaces it keeps the property the floor was put there for: the knob's low end must still read as country rather than as a clean guitar. Check the same value on genre 5's `_ =>` case while here — pop reaches that branch by elimination and its knob is `GLIDE`, so its rate is not this rate. Re-bless; `--levels` does not move. |

| 5 | The live site is one build, so comparing two of them is a local rebuild nobody else can hear | **The problem is structural, not a missing feature.** There is one Pages site per repo and `deploy-pages` replaces the entire tree, so the `preview/*` mechanism can only put a branch on the site *instead of* master — never beside it. Meanwhile audio is expected to change from commit to commit (see the header), which makes "does this sound better than what we had" the question asked most often about this repo and the one that costs the most to answer: build both locally, listen twice, and have no way at all to hand the comparison to someone else. **The shape: one artifact, many trees.** A job running from MASTER's workflow checks out each selected ref, runs that ref's own `make dist`, and assembles master at `/` plus each branch at `/b/<branch>/`. Two existing properties are what make it cheap rather than a rewrite — every path in `web/` is relative (already a rule, precisely so the project-page subpath needs no rewriting), so a tree works unmodified at any depth; and the deploy packages the committed `web/_framework` rather than compiling it, so a branch costs a checkout and a copy, not a two-minute AOT publish. Per-branch trees skip `dist/skafinity.html`: the ~9.5 MiB single-file handout belongs at the root only, and dropping it roughly halves a branch to ~10 MiB. **`make check-bundle` runs per branch, and a FAILURE must render a placeholder page for that branch** rather than failing the whole deploy or — far worse — letting `/b/<name>/` serve a tree whose glue and bundle disagree. That is the same stale-bundle failure the gate already exists for, except a branch URL makes it invisible in a second way: it looks like the branch, so a wrong render reads as the branch's fault. **The container is the point, and it is narrower than "tabs".** Master's root gains a picker driven by a `branches.json` the job writes (name, sha, subject, date, stamp kind, ok/stale), and it must SHOW THE SHA — the entire purpose is knowing which build is making the noise. Its real job is to hold `#vibe:tag:n` constant across a switch so the seed stays put while the engine under it changes: that is the A/B, and it is what makes this a listening tool rather than a directory index. The digests say whether a render moved; only an ear says whether it improved. Load ONE iframe at a time and destroy it on switch — each tree boots its own .NET runtime plus a worker and holds a whole song of PCM, so a live tab per branch is several runtimes idling. A true side-by-side (two runtimes, one audible, instant switch) is the tempting next step, doubles the memory, and is a separate decision. **Decide the branch set before building it:** all branches is unbounded, open-PR branches auto-track work in flight at the cost of an API call, `preview/*` is bounded and reuses a convention that already exists. `preview/*` is the recommendation — the prefix stops meaning "replaces the site" and starts meaning "gets a subdirectory", which is strictly less surprising than what it means today. **This RETIRES the preview-replaces-the-site rule and CLAUDE.md changes in the same PR**: the site becomes master at `/` always, the "a site that does not match master is not by itself a stale bundle" caveat goes away, and the `github-pages` deployment branch policy goes back to `master` alone. That last part is also a small security win — today a `preview/*` branch runs its OWN workflow file holding `pages: write`, and under this row the only workflow that ever deploys is master's. Give the assembling job `permissions: contents: read` while there: it runs `make` out of other branches' checkouts and should not be holding the deploy token when it does. **Two non-starters, so they are not re-derived later:** a `gh-pages` accumulator branch (deleted branches leave dead trees forever, and the repo grows ~10 MiB per push), and swapping only `_framework` under a single page — bundle-matches-glue means a version is the whole tree, not a hot-swappable runtime. No engine change, no re-bless; `--levels` does not move. |

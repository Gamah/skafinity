# PLAN

**Everything here must keep working in s&box.** The engine is shared source, not a port:
`sbox-library/Skafinity/Code/Engine/**` compiles into both the s&box library and the web wasm bundle.
So every row below is bound by the same rules — the engine stays framework-free (`System`,
`System.Collections.Generic`, `System.Text` only; no `Sandbox.*`, no Emscripten-isms), anything
web-specific goes in `wasm/Exports.cs`, and any new `Config` field lands in `Cfg.To`/`Cfg.From`
(+ `Cfg.Size`) or it won't survive the JS boundary. `SkafinityPlayer.cs` and `Code/UI/` are
s&box-only and must still compile and drive the engine after each row.

Seed compatibility is **not** preserved: old `vibe:tag:n` seeds are expected to break, and the
audio a seed renders is expected to change from commit to commit. That is accepted — no
back-compat shim, no golden-audio contract, and a changed render is never a regression. The
only reproducibility that matters is *within* a build (web and s&box agreeing, and a pure
refactor proving it moved nothing).

| 30 | The genre roster is six, and the obvious missing ones are the cousins of the genres already here | Wanted: **two-tone** and **reggae** as genres in their own right, and an exploration of whether **rock** and **pop** are each really one genre. All four are the same shape of question — the current six are broad umbrellas, and the retune of genre 0 showed that an umbrella genre gets tuned to exactly one era by accident and then reads as mistuned to everyone who meant a different one. Two-tone is the direct case: it is neither the first-wave shuffle nor the ska-punk blast, it is the fast straight 2 Tone sound with its own harmony and its own production, and today there is nowhere for it. Reggae is the other end of the same lineage (one-drop, swung hats, a much slower band — 12/8 makes it partly dependent on the non-4/4 row) and is where the *current* ska profile's melodic bass, roomy mix and laid-back form would find their proper home, so the retune's discarded tables are the seed of it rather than a loss. Rock and pop are the umbrellas most likely to be hiding two distinct genres each; work out what the split actually is before assuming it (the tempo-band study row is the same kind of exercise and should probably be done first, since it is the tool for telling two eras apart). **The mechanism is append-only and cheap** — genre is the first base-36 char, so 36 genres fit with no wire change; `GenreProfile.Profiles` is an array indexed by genre id and appends without shifting anything; each genre's instrument grid is already independent. **The hard part is the anti-overlap rule, and it gets combinatorially worse.** CLAUDE.md caps any two genres at one shared progression and one shared scale, and the suite asserts it — six genres already required pruning I–V–vi–IV out of four tables. Close cousins are exactly the case that rule punishes: ska-punk and two-tone, or two halves of a split rock, will collide on the first table anyone writes. Before adding a genre, decide whether the cap survives as-is, becomes a per-pair rule (cousins may overlap more, distant genres may not), or is replaced by a distinctness measure over the whole profile rather than over the harmony tables alone — because the real answer is that two-tone differs from ska-punk in feel, form, production and horn writing rather than in its chord choices, and a rule that only watches harmony cannot see that. Each new genre is a full table set (scales, progressions, voicings, bass, comp figures, keys figures, grooves, form, lead style, accents, mix) plus a `--levels` pass to measure its balances. Re-bless the digests only if the matrix is extended to cover the new genres — adding a profile past the end of the array moves nothing that exists. |
| 28 | There is no actual BEND in the engine — only a lean into pitch, and its depth is not a parameter | `Expression.cs` rolls four gestures and the two pitch ones are hardcoded: `BendIn` sets `BendSemis` to −0.3 or −0.55 semitones over 90 ms, `Scoop` sets `ScoopSemis` to 0.15 or 0.3. Those are *approach* gestures — a singer or a horn easing onto the note — and they are right for what they are. What is missing is the guitar bend a listener would name as one: a **whole step or a semitone, bent up and HELD**, or bent up to the chord tone above and released, which is a different musical event and lands in the middle of the note rather than at its front. The timing side is already correct (`BendTime` is in SECONDS, so a bend does not scale with tempo — keep it that way). The gap is that depth is fixed while only the *propensity* is configurable (`LeadGtrBend` → `Expr()`), so a genre cannot say "bends deep" vs "bends barely", and there is no target-pitch bend at all. Wants a `BendSemis` range on the propensity struct (or a separate depth field) plus a held-bend variant in `Voicing`, and the synth already reads `BendSemis`/`BendTime`, so the render side may need nothing. Interacts with the country row below — that row is about *how often* country bends, and it is hard to judge frequency while every bend is the same shallow one. Re-bless; `--levels` unaffected. |
| 25 | Voice leading never reassigns chord tones between voices, so a stepwise root move still slides the whole shape | `Harmony.PlanVoiceLeading` octave-shifts each voice to the inversion nearest the chord before it, which killed the octave-sized parallel leap — but voice *i* is always the same degree offset of the voicing, so a root move of a fourth (I→IV, under a tritone already) moves every voice by that fourth. No common tone, just a smaller parallel slide, which is the other half of "the comp sounds like one part rather than a chord calculator". The fix is to let the chord ROTATE: voice *i* plays voicing offset `(i + r) mod n`, with the rotation chosen per chord by the same cycle-closing walk that picks the octaves (they compose — a rotation is chosen alongside an octave, not instead of it). The obstacle is that the pitch array's INDEX currently means "voicing slot": `DrivenVoicing` in `Guitar.cs` drops the third by testing `_voicing[i]` against the *i*th pitch, so a rotated chord must carry its offsets alongside its pitches or the driven guitar starts dropping the wrong note. Watch the strum too — `RenderStrumBar` nudges `tones[i]` by `i * 4 ms`, so array order is the order the pick crosses the strings. Re-measure `--levels`: moving which voice is on top moves the comp's average register. |

That is a judgement made without listening to it — the last chord of every song can still jump a seventh from the chord before it, which is exactly the "it jumped" the voice-leading row was about, sitting on the most exposed moment in the song.

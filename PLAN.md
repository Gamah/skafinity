# PLAN

**Everything here must keep working in s&box.** The engine is shared source, not a port:
`sbox-library/Skafinity/Code/Engine/**` compiles into both the s&box library and the web wasm bundle.
So every row below is bound by the same rules — the engine stays framework-free (`System`,
`System.Collections.Generic`, `System.Text` only; no `Sandbox.*`, no Emscripten-isms), anything
web-specific goes in `wasm/Exports.cs`, and any new `Config` field lands in `Cfg.To`/`Cfg.From`
(+ `Cfg.Size`) or it won't survive the JS boundary. `SkafinityPlayer.cs` and `Code/UI/` are
s&box-only and must still compile and drive the engine after each row.

Seed compatibility is **not** preserved: old `vibe:tag:n` seeds are expected to break, and the
audio a seed renders is expected to change from commit to commit. That is accepted — no
back-compat shim, no golden-audio contract, and a changed render is never a regression. The
only reproducibility that matters is *within* a build (web and s&box agreeing, and a pure
refactor proving it moved nothing).

| Rank | Item | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 25 | Voice leading never reassigns chord tones between voices, so a stepwise root move still slides the whole shape | `Harmony.PlanVoiceLeading` octave-shifts each voice to the inversion nearest the chord before it, which killed the octave-sized parallel leap — but voice *i* is always the same degree offset of the voicing, so a root move of a fourth (I→IV, under a tritone already) moves every voice by that fourth. No common tone, just a smaller parallel slide, which is the other half of "the comp sounds like one part rather than a chord calculator". The fix is to let the chord ROTATE: voice *i* plays voicing offset `(i + r) mod n`, with the rotation chosen per chord by the same cycle-closing walk that picks the octaves (they compose — a rotation is chosen alongside an octave, not instead of it). The obstacle is that the pitch array's INDEX currently means "voicing slot": `DrivenVoicing` in `Guitar.cs` drops the third by testing `_voicing[i]` against the *i*th pitch, so a rotated chord must carry its offsets alongside its pitches or the driven guitar starts dropping the wrong note. Watch the strum too — `RenderStrumBar` nudges `tones[i]` by `i * 4 ms`, so array order is the order the pick crosses the strings. Re-measure `--levels`: moving which voice is on top moves the comp's average register. |
| 25 | The ending chord is the one place a comp still leaps | `EndingChord` builds from `VoicedMidis` (root position) rather than through `ChordMidis`, deliberately: a song should land where the genre voices its final chord, not where the last change happened to leave the register. That is a judgement made without listening to it — the last chord of every song can still jump a seventh from the chord before it, which is exactly the "it jumped" the voice-leading row was about, sitting on the most exposed moment in the song. Listen to a few endings per genre (`--score <seed>` over the last bars against `--seed`'s `voicelead` line) and decide: keep it anchored, voice-lead it like any other change, or voice-lead it and let the ritard cover the move. Cheap either way — it is one call site. |
| 25 | Country's lead bends into half its notes and scoops half of them | `Expr("LEAD GTR")` gives genre 2 `BendIn` and `Scoop` of at least 0.5 each (`MathF.Max( _c.LeadGtrBend, 0.5f )`, both propensities off the same value), against 0.30 vibrato. That was set when country's lead was dyads, where the parallel bending was the thing that read as a horn; the pair is gone, so the propensity is now exposed on a single line and nothing has judged it on its own. If country's lead reads seasick rather than twangy, this is the knob and not the notes — and note `BendIn` and `Scoop` do not have to move together, a bend UP INTO the note is the telecaster gesture and a scoop within it is a different one. Purely a listening call: change the numbers, `--levels` is unaffected, re-bless. |
| 25 | 211 chord changes still move a voice more than a tritone | Down from 765 in root position, and every survivor is optimal for the CYCLE — one wider move that buys smaller ones on the other three changes, which is the solution rather than a failure of it (the engine test asserts the count, not that it is zero). Unlistened-to: the maths says these are the right trade and only an ear says whether a single voice jumping a minor sixth while the others hold reads as a re-voicing or as a fault. `--score` over a seed whose `voicelead` line is non-zero is the way in. If they do read badly the lever is the cost function in `PlanVoiceLeading` — weighting the WORST edge rather than the total would spread the motion, at the price of moving more voices on average. |
| 14 | Non-4/4 | `Timing` already carries `BeatsPerBar` and a `BeatGrouping` array (nothing reads the grouping yet), `ComposePlan` picks the meter in one place, and patterns are meter-agnostic now — a `Pattern` carries its own tick length and nothing in a voice assumes eight cells to a bar. 6/8 and 12/8 first (12/8 is the natural home of a one-drop shuffle; a country bass lesson in 6/8 turned up in the research). Compound meter wants a `BeatTicks` on `Timing` (48 simple, 72 compound) rather than a second grid. Changing meter *within* a song is the section map's job — `Part` already carries per-bar beat counts for anomalous measures, so it is the same mechanism widened. |
